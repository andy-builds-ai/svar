# Pellwick Joinery — Window and Door Care and Our Guarantee

*Given to every customer after fitting. Keep it with your house papers.*

Timber windows and doors last for generations when they are looked after.
The care is simple, but it has to happen. This leaflet explains what to do,
and what our guarantee covers.

## Our guarantee

| What | Covered for | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Timber against rot and fungal decay | **10 years** | Windows and external doors made by us, in larch or oak |
| Double-glazed units against misting between the panes | **5 years** | New sealed units supplied by us |
| Workmanship: joints, fitting, hanging | **2 years** | Anything we made or fitted |
| Hardware: hinges, locks, handles, stays | **2 years** | Parts supplied by us |
| Repairs and sash overhauls | **1 year** | The parts we repaired or replaced |

The guarantee starts on the day fitting is complete, applies once the
invoice is paid in full (see *Quotes, Prices and Payment*), and moves with
the house if you sell it.

### Conditions

The 10-year timber guarantee needs the paint or oil finish to be
maintained as described below. We ask to see that it has been, for example
by photographs or a receipt for decorating.

### Not covered

- Damage from lack of maintenance, especially cracked paint left open.
- Paint systems from other makers applied over ours without preparation.
- Normal movement of timber: small hairline cracks in the paint at joints
  are normal in the first year and are not a defect.
- Damage from building work, accidents or forced entry.
- Condensation on the room side of the glass, which is a ventilation matter.
- Windows and doors fitted by others.

## Your windows in the first year

- **Hairline cracks** at joints may appear as the timber settles. Touch them
  up with the matching paint we leave with you.
- **Doors may bind slightly** in wet weather. Do not plane them; call us.
  Adjustments in the first year are free.
- **Book a free check visit** in the first year if you like. Rory will
  adjust hinges and locks and look at the finish.

## Painted windows and doors

We finish larch windows and doors in the workshop with a microporous paint
system: one primer, two undercoats and one top coat. This lets moisture
out of the timber but keeps rain out.

**Repaint every 5 to 7 years** on sheltered sides, and every **4 to 5 years**
on sides facing the prevailing wind and rain. Signs that it is time:

- the paint looks chalky when you rub it,
- small cracks do not close again in dry weather,
- the paint has worn through on the bottom rail or the sill.

How to repaint:

1. Wash down with warm water and a little washing-up liquid; let dry.
2. Sand lightly to take off the gloss. Do not strip sound paint.
3. Where bare timber shows, prime it first.
4. Apply one undercoat and one top coat of a microporous paint. Ask us for
   the colour code; we keep it on file.
5. Paint on a dry day above 8 °C, not in direct midday sun.

## Oiled oak doors

Oak doors are finished with a hard wax oil.

- **Re-oil once a year**, ideally in late spring, on the outside face.
- Clean with a damp cloth, let dry, apply a thin coat of the same oil with a
  lint-free cloth and wipe off the excess after 15 minutes.
- Grey patches mean the oil has worn; re-oil sooner.
- Never use varnish on an oiled door; it will peel.

## Sash windows

- Keep the pulleys free of paint.
- Once a year, rub a little candle wax on the sides of the sashes and the
  staff beads so they run smoothly.
- If a sash cord breaks, stop using that sash and call us; a hanging sash
  on one cord can fall.

## Hardware

- Oil hinges and friction stays twice a year with light machine oil.
- Lubricate multi-point locks on doors once a year with a lock spray, never
  with grease.
- If a key becomes hard to turn, do not force it. The door usually needs a
  small adjustment.

## Glass

Clean with warm water or a glass cleaner. Do not use scrapers or scouring
pads on the seals. If misting appears **between** the panes, the sealed unit
has failed; within 5 years it is replaced under guarantee.
